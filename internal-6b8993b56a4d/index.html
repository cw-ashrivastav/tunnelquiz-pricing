Top-ups

Two prices. They never change and never expire.

Credits

₹15 each + GST

Any quantity from 50. Same price on every paid plan. Buy 200, pay ₹3,000. Purchased credits never expire and are spent after your monthly allowance.

AI credits

₹65 each + GST

Any quantity from 10, on Pro and Business. One AI credit writes about 150 questions or runs a handful of chat interviews. Never expires.

Credits carry forward

Unused plan credits roll over for three months while you're subscribed. If you cancel, your balance is frozen - not lost - and comes back the day you resubscribe.

Free credits reset

The Free plan's 50 credits arrive on the 1st and expire at month end. They can't be banked, and they aren't used while a paid plan is active.

Nothing charged on invite

A credit is spent only when a candidate actually starts a paper. Invitations, links and unstarted tests cost nothing.

Grading is the only real variable cost in the product. Where these numbers could be wrong

  1. AI grading share is an allowance, not a measurement. The 40% assumption for Pro is the largest unmeasured input here - ₹168 of ₹635. If Pro customers write mostly written and coding papers rather than MCQ it doubles, and Pro's margin drops to about 77%. Instrument grading calls per attempt before scaling Pro.
  2. Nothing meters grading or chat tokens today. Generation is fully metered against AI credits with real provider token counts; grading and chat are not - the engine's estimator is len(text) // 4 and grading jobs record no cost. Every grading figure above is modelled.
  3. Retry amplification is in no figure. A job-level grading retry re-grades all N questions when one fails, up to three times.
  4. Live Follow bandwidth has never been measured. ₹50 assumes 20 hour-long sessions. It is priced at zero credits on the argument that a human must watch and so it is self-limiting; if that fails it becomes a real line.
  5. The AI-credit redundancy allowance rests on one 45-item run. 165,000 tokens per credit assumes a 15.6% duplicate rate; the human reviewer discarded only 6.7%.
  6. Face-capture photos are kept indefinitely - excluded from the 90-day lifecycle rule. Trivial per attempt, unbounded in aggregate, and a biometric retention exposure independent of cost.
  7. The 90-day retention rule is set by hand in the Cloudflare dashboard, not in code. Remove it and storage grows linearly and silently.
  8. The Free tier now stores media. Screen capture on Free takes it from ₹2 to ₹21 an org a month - ₹210,000 at 10,000 free orgs. Still a marketing line, but it scales with signups rather than revenue, and it is the one cost here that a successful free campaign makes worse. Watch it against signup volume.
  9. Fixed infrastructure is not attributed at all. These are marginal costs. Two Redis instances, the engine, the backend, two workers and Mongo are paid for regardless, and at low volume they dominate the bill. A 90% marginal margin does not mean the business is profitable at ten customers.
